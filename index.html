Private Client Insurance Replacement

A Second Growth Engine for Private Client Mobility

Go Rentals already has the difficult part: a hospitality-led service model and trusted relationships capable of delivering a premium experience after a loss. The growth opportunity is to preserve that engine while moving the customer relationship upstream — before the referral decision ever occurs.
Strategic unlock: protect what already works, move acquisition upstream, and turn a post-loss referral into a pre-established luxury-mobility relationship that can be activated at the moment of need.
What the business analysis reveals
The opportunity is not to replace Go’s current model. It is to make it compound.
The same relationship discipline that makes Go valuable in aviation, hospitality and Private Client service can be applied earlier in insurance replacement — shifting Go from one of several post-loss choices to an already-established part of the luxury ownership experience.
Hospitality is the differentiator The vehicle matters, but the defensible value is removing friction and protecting the guest experience for both the customer and the referring partner.
Referral timing is the constraint Today Go can deliver an exceptional experience once the guest reaches Go, but acquisition still begins after a loss and depends on another party’s referral.
Earlier ownership creates leverage If Go is selected when the luxury vehicle is acquired, the existing carrier, broker and collision ecosystem can support an enrolled customer rather than compete to originate one.

How it works — without turning Go into a technology company

The model does not require Go to continuously track a driver or rebuild an insurance claims system. It needs a small number of trusted connections that recognize the customer, recognize the event and start the hospitality workflow.
1
Establish the relationship at purchaseThe customer selects the premium mobility benefit. Go receives the minimum information needed to recognize the customer later: contact details, VIN, vehicle class, benefit terms and permission to act.
2
The vehicle recognizes a qualifying collisionThe connected vehicle already knows its VIN and can detect certain crash events. Instead of sending Go a stream of driving behavior, an authorized event message can say, in effect: “this enrolled vehicle may have had a qualifying loss.”
3
Match, verify and open the mobility workflowThe VIN links the event back to the enrolled benefit. Simple rules confirm the benefit is active and route an alert to Go. A person can still confirm the guest, location and need before a vehicle is dispatched.
4
Claim and mobility run in parallelThe carrier continues handling coverage, liability and the claim. Go handles mobility. If connected, the claim number, rental status and repair timing can flow between the parties instead of being re-keyed across phone calls and emails.
5
Deliver the Go experience and close the loopGo confirms the right vehicle, delivery point and guest needs. Repair status can help manage extensions and return timing. The completed event becomes useful operating data for forecasting demand and improving the program.

The technical logic, in plain English

VIN is the common key The Vehicle Identification Number can connect the purchase record, connected-car event, insurance claim and replacement-mobility benefit without needing a large amount of behavioral data.
Event-based, not surveillance-based Go does not need constant driving history. It needs an authorized message when a qualifying event occurs, plus enough information to identify and help the guest.
Connections can be simple at first A pilot can begin with secure system-to-system messages and human confirmation. Deeper carrier and repair integrations can be added after the economics and guest experience are proven.
Automation starts the work; hospitality finishes it The system can recognize and route the event. A Go team member still owns the judgment, communication and premium guest experience.
Exceptions always need a human path No signal, false alert, inactive benefit, unusual repair situation or unavailable vehicle should break the experience. Those cases fall back to a person instead of failing silently.
Important distinction: the technology is only the connective tissue. Go’s competitive advantage remains the relationship and hospitality layer.

What information actually has to move

Enough to act — not everything the vehicle or insurer knows.
At enrollment
Customer/contact, VIN, make/model, agreed replacement class, benefit term and consent.
At the event
VIN, qualifying collision flag, time and — with permission — location; optional severity if useful.
From the claim
Claim number, carrier/adjuster contact, relevant rental status and repair timing when available.
For fulfillment
Guest confirmation, vehicle requirement, delivery location, expected duration and preferred repair facility if applicable.

Business case: proven engine vs. embedded growth engine

The case for change is additive, not disruptive: preserve the relationship engine that produces business today, while testing whether upstream enrollment can create a more predictable source of future demand.

Current referral model

Proven / lower change
Advantages
  • Fits today’s carrier, broker and collision workflows.
  • Can grow incrementally without creating a new consumer product.
  • Uses relationships Go already knows how to build and service.
  • Lower technology, regulatory and implementation burden.
  • Partners retain flexibility to choose Go claim by claim.
Constraints
  • Go enters after the loss, when the customer can already be captured elsewhere.
  • Growth depends heavily on referral behavior and partner activation.
  • Multiple handoffs create friction and inconsistent customer experience.
  • Volume can be harder to forecast because Go does not own the customer relationship in advance.
  • Scaling often requires winning accounts and locations one relationship at a time.

Embedded luxury-mobility model

Scalable / higher build
Advantages
  • Moves customer acquisition to vehicle purchase or lease, before a loss.
  • Reduces referral leakage because Go is already the selected mobility provider.
  • Connected-car detection can turn a loss into an activation event rather than a new sales decision.
  • Creates an enrolled customer base that may improve fleet and geographic demand forecasting.
  • Connects dealer/OEM, carrier, broker and collision relationships around one customer.
  • Creates the possibility of reciprocal preferred collision partnerships.
  • Extends Go’s hospitality promise across the full luxury ownership experience.
Execution risks / questions
  • Product structure may require a licensed risk-bearing partner depending on how the benefit is designed.
  • OEM telematics access, customer consent and privacy controls must be negotiated.
  • Dealer distribution must be compelling enough to earn point-of-sale adoption.
  • Carrier/broker workflows must clearly recognize the separate mobility benefit and avoid coverage confusion.
  • Go must have the right vehicle available when an automated activation occurs.
  • False alerts, connectivity failures and non-qualifying events need fallback processes.
  • Multi-state compliance and partner integrations make rollout more complex than referral selling.
Key business conclusion: the embedded model has greater implementation complexity, but it attacks the biggest structural weakness in the current model — Go does not control the acquisition point. If validated, it can convert a variable post-loss referral into a pre-established customer relationship while keeping the existing carrier, broker and collision channels valuable.

Growth path: preserve → prove → scale

A practical rollout would protect current revenue while proving whether the new acquisition model produces better economics and experience.

1. Current engine Keep expanding carriers, brokers, OEM-certified collision centers and MSOs. This continues generating referral revenue now.
→
2. Controlled pilot One luxury dealer/OEM ecosystem + one market + one product/risk partner + connected-car event activation. Measure adoption, claims, rental days and guest experience.
→
3. Embedded growth engine Scale only what proves out: additional dealers/OEMs, carriers, states and preferred collision partners — while the original referral channel remains active.
Why this matters: Go does not have to choose between Greg’s current strategy and the new model. The current network can generate business today and also become the operating network that fulfills an embedded customer base tomorrow.
Conceptual model. “Embedded” means Go is pre-established as the selected premium replacement-mobility provider and can receive an authorized qualifying-event notification. A practical implementation would use only the data needed to identify, validate and serve the guest, with human confirmation and fallback paths built in. Product structure, customer consent, OEM/connected-vehicle access, carrier participation and state regulation would determine the final design. OEM = Original Equipment Manufacturer. FNOL = First Notice of Loss. VIN = Vehicle Identification Number. MSO = Multi-Shop Operator.
