Private Client Insurance Replacement

From Referral-Based Rental to Embedded Luxury Mobility

A strategic ecosystem model built after first understanding Go Rentals’ hospitality model, current insurance-replacement workflow and the friction surrounding the Private Client guest — then asking where the relationship could move earlier in the journey.
Core idea: the technology is not the product. The product is the pre-established premium mobility relationship. Connected-car accident detection is the activation mechanism.

The assignment I heard

Understand Go Rentals beyond the vehicle, understand the guest experience and insurance-replacement opportunity, then think about how I could hit the ground running using the experience and relationship instincts I already bring.

1. Understand the business Start with Go as a hospitality-first mobility company — not simply a premium rental fleet.
2. Understand the experience Look at where a Private Client guest experiences friction after a loss and what Go protects for the guest and referring partner.
3. Find the opportunity Map the existing carrier, broker and collision ecosystem, then look for a scalable “opportunity within the opportunity.”
What followed: I first mapped the current ecosystem. The proposed model is the working hypothesis that came out of that analysis — something to validate against Go’s actual operating experience, not an assumption that I already know the business from the outside.

Business case: current model vs. embedded model

The strongest case is not that the new model makes the current one obsolete. It is that Go can preserve the existing relationship engine while adding a more predictable, upstream acquisition engine.

Current referral model

Proven / lower change
Advantages
  • Fits today’s carrier, broker and collision workflows.
  • Can grow incrementally without creating a new consumer product.
  • Uses relationships Go already knows how to build and service.
  • Lower technology, regulatory and implementation burden.
  • Partners retain flexibility to choose Go claim by claim.
Constraints
  • Go enters after the loss, when the customer can already be captured elsewhere.
  • Growth depends heavily on referral behavior and partner activation.
  • Multiple handoffs create friction and inconsistent customer experience.
  • Volume can be harder to forecast because Go does not own the customer relationship in advance.
  • Scaling often requires winning accounts and locations one relationship at a time.

Embedded luxury-mobility model

Scalable / higher build
Advantages
  • Moves customer acquisition to vehicle purchase or lease, before a loss.
  • Reduces referral leakage because Go is already the selected mobility provider.
  • Connected-car detection can turn a loss into an activation event rather than a new sales decision.
  • Creates an enrolled customer base that may improve fleet and geographic demand forecasting.
  • Connects dealer/OEM, carrier, broker and collision relationships around one customer.
  • Creates the possibility of reciprocal preferred collision partnerships.
  • Extends Go’s hospitality promise across the full luxury ownership experience.
Execution risks / questions
  • Product structure may require a licensed risk-bearing partner depending on how the benefit is designed.
  • OEM telematics access, customer consent and privacy controls must be negotiated.
  • Dealer distribution must be compelling enough to earn point-of-sale adoption.
  • Carrier/broker workflows must clearly recognize the separate mobility benefit and avoid coverage confusion.
  • Go must have the right vehicle available when an automated activation occurs.
  • False alerts, connectivity failures and non-qualifying events need fallback processes.
  • Multi-state compliance and partner integrations make rollout more complex than referral selling.
Key business conclusion: the embedded model has greater implementation complexity, but it attacks the biggest structural weakness in the current model — Go does not control the acquisition point. If validated, it can convert a variable post-loss referral into a pre-established customer relationship while keeping the existing carrier, broker and collision channels valuable.

Streamlined growth path

A practical rollout would protect current revenue while proving whether the new acquisition model produces better economics and experience.

1. Current engine Keep expanding carriers, brokers, OEM-certified collision centers and MSOs. This continues generating referral revenue now.
→
2. Controlled pilot One luxury dealer/OEM ecosystem + one market + one product/risk partner + connected-car event activation. Measure adoption, claims, rental days and guest experience.
→
3. Embedded growth engine Scale only what proves out: additional dealers/OEMs, carriers, states and preferred collision partners — while the original referral channel remains active.
Why this matters: Go does not have to choose between Greg’s current strategy and the new model. The current network can generate business today and also become the operating network that fulfills an embedded customer base tomorrow.
Conceptual model. “Embedded” means Go is pre-established as the selected premium replacement-mobility provider and receives an authorized qualifying-event notification. Actual implementation would depend on product structure, customer consent, OEM/telematics access, carrier participation, state regulation, connectivity and event validation. OEM = Original Equipment Manufacturer. FNOL = First Notice of Loss. VIN = Vehicle Identification Number. MSO = Multi-Shop Operator.
